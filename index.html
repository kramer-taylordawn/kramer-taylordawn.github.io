SureStride: Engineering Capstone Project

SolidWorks FEA Python 3D Printing Rapid Prototyping Manufacturing

The Problem

Seniors in nursing homes who use assistive mobility devices (AMDs) need an intermediate device that improves mobility without the stability limits of a walker or the autonomy limits of a wheelchair.

The Impact

Our design aims to increase autonomy and reduce fall risk, improving activity levels and quality of life for older adults.

The Solution

Mechatronic walker with automatic electrical & mechanical resistance braking, size adjustability, and improved upper-body support.

Preliminary Design Iteration

Early collapsible prototypes proved unstable — we removed the collapsible mechanism after stakeholder feedback and rapid prototyping lessons.

Preliminary CAD & PLA prototype

Final Mechanical Outcomes

The mechanical prototype uses aluminum tubing, size adjustability in five locations, mechanical disk brakes, and front swivel wheels.

Final SolidWorks CAD & manufactured assembly

Mechanical Validation

FEA validated loading up to 100 kg — mesh and stress plots confirmed safe regions at critical bends and holes.

Final Electrical Outcomes

ESP32-based control with sensors (accelerometer, ToF) to detect wobble, speed and obstacles. Linear actuators provide graded braking; LED feedback indicates system state.

Electrical layout and component placement

Electrical Validation

Bench tests confirmed correct operation of switches, LEDs, actuators, converter, and reliable sensor measurements for wobble and distance detection.

Final Result

Our prototype demonstrated full structural safety under load testing, reliable electrical performance, and met primary design objectives for stability, adjustability and usability.

🏆 Selected for presentation at the iBioMed Project Showcase, recognizing the team’s innovation and technical rigor.

Personal Contributions

I led mechanical validation and CAD design, supported test plan creation, and contributed to manufacturing and group reporting.

BoundLess Crutches: Pre-Engineering Capstone Project

Autodesk InventorMATLABPrototypingHand Calculations

The Problem

Conventional crutches can cause nerve compression injuries and are awkward to transport; we sought a portable alternative that distributes load more safely.

Mechanical Outcomes

Aluminum tubing, adjustable geometry, and tested loading validated the final concept. FEA confirmed safe stress under expected loads.

Controls / Other

Design includes options for simple sensor feedback for future iterations.

The design reduced peak shoulder loading in simulations and was improved through rapid prototyping and stakeholder feedback.
